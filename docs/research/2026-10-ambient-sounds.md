# Ambient sound channels: rain, wind, waves (researched 2026-10-26)

All sources are SEO vendor blogs, channel-stat aggregators, and sound-pack stores. Confidence is low to medium. No RPM figure specific to this format was found.

## Findings

- **Crowded:** rain-sound videos are described as plentiful, so standing out is hard. (medium)
- **Repetitive-content risk:** a marketing blog reports ambient and lo-fi channels have some of the highest YPP rejection rates for repetitive content, because long continuous tracks and recycled visuals look templated. Review is described as judging the whole channel. Unconfirmed by YouTube's own documentation. (medium-low)
- **Mitigation named in sources:** distinct visuals per video, original recordings, and proof of recording such as behind-the-scenes footage. (medium-low)
- **Search behavior:** long-tail queries that include a duration (hours) and a sound signal viewers who leave it playing all night. Strong title pairing: a sound type plus a sleep outcome. (low, vendor analysis)
- **Small-channel earnings:** one aggregator page shows about 275 subscribers, 615K views, and estimated $0-3 per day. One data point, not a benchmark. (low)
- **Licensing:** commercial sound libraries (for example TunePocket) sell rain and ocean loops for commercial use; their free previews are explicitly unlicensed. Freesound license terms (CC0 versus attribution) were not verified and must be checked per clip. (medium for library claim, unverified for Freesound)

## Implications

- Cheap to make, but the cheapest version (one loop, many titles) is the version most likely to be rejected.
- Provenance and distinctness are the product.
- Treat as a test arm, not a primary bet.
